1. Start with a baseline, not a goal

Before any targets, you need a clear, defensible baseline:
	•	Total emissions (Scope 1, 2, and ideally Scope 3)
	•	Energy consumption (kWh by site, asset, or function)
	•	Activity drivers:
	•	Fleet km travelled
	•	Fuel usage
	•	Electricity by building
	•	Refrigerants, waste, etc.

This becomes your “line in the sand” year.

👉 Without this, every goal is meaningless.

⸻

2. Segment the business (this is where most fail)

Instead of “one company target”, break it down:

Operational Segments
	•	Buildings / facilities (HVAC, lighting)
	•	Critical infrastructure (your exchanges, DC environments)
	•	Fleet / transport
	•	Supply chain (if you go Scope 3)

Why this matters:

Each segment has different physics and different levers.

Example:
	•	HVAC optimisation → fast wins
	•	Fleet electrification → slower, capex-heavy
	•	Supply chain → influence, not control

⸻

3. Use a maturity model

You don’t set the same targets for a company at Level 1 vs Level 4.

Simple 5-level model:

Level 1 – Unknown
	•	No reliable data
	•	Estimates only

Level 2 – Measured
	•	Annual footprint calculated
	•	Spreadsheet-driven

Level 3 – Managed
	•	Regular tracking (monthly/quarterly)
	•	Some reduction initiatives

Level 4 – Optimised
	•	Near real-time data
	•	Active optimisation (this is your AssetIQ sweet spot)

Level 5 – Autonomous / Regenerative
	•	AI-driven decisions
	•	Continuous optimisation + offset strategy

👉 The level determines:
	•	How aggressive your targets can be
	•	Whether you focus on data first or reduction first

⸻

4. Define the reduction levers

This is where it becomes real.

Each segment gets mapped to levers:

Example levers:
	•	HVAC optimisation (temperature, airflow, containment)
	•	Solar + energy sourcing
	•	Equipment efficiency upgrades
	•	Behaviour change (low impact but fast)
	•	Fleet transition
	•	Load shifting / demand response

Each lever should have:
	•	Estimated % reduction
	•	Cost (capex/opex)
	•	Time to implement
	•	Confidence level

⸻

5. Build a scenario model

Instead of one goal, you create scenarios:

Example:
	•	Conservative: 10–15% reduction over 3 years
	•	Realistic: 25–35%
	•	Aggressive: 50%+

Each scenario is a combination of levers.

👉 This is exactly where your thinking shines:
This becomes an operational decision engine, not a report.

⸻

6. Align with global frameworks (so it’s credible)

You don’t need to be boxed in by them, but you should align with:
	•	Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)
	•	GHG Protocol
	•	Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures

This ensures:
	•	Board confidence
	•	Investor alignment
	•	Tender credibility

⸻

7. Convert into clear targets

Now you can actually set goals:

Types of targets:
	•	Absolute reduction
→ “Reduce emissions by 30% by 2030”
	•	Intensity-based
→ “Reduce emissions per m² or per unit of output”
	•	Operational targets
→ “Increase HVAC setpoint to 24°C across 80% of sites”

👉 The best companies use a mix of all three

⸻

8. Make it operational (this is your edge)

This is where most ESG programmes die.

You flip it from:

Reporting what happened

To:

Controlling what happens

That means:
	•	Real-time dashboards
	•	Alerts on inefficiency (cooling leaks, idle energy use)
	•	Automated recommendations (or actions)

This is literally:
👉 AssetIQ / EnviroIQ layer
